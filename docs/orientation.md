# Finance AI Lab — Orientation

## What this is

A biweekly meeting that combines friendship, career/business conversation, structured learning, and a small shared engineering lab.

The structure exists to prevent two failure modes:

1. the meeting becomes only life updates and nothing compounds;
2. the meeting becomes so formal that it stops being enjoyable.

## Four tracks

### Financial systems
Market structure, clearing, settlement, custody, collateral, payments, tokenization, operational risk, standards.

### Markets and economics
Equities, rates, credit, commodities, FX, volatility, inflation, labor, liquidity, earnings, valuation.

### AI and process engineering
Coding agents, tool-using agents, context engineering, evaluations, observability, human approvals, security, process redesign, inference economics.

### Building
Market tools, research assistants, data pipelines, small automations, notebooks, explainers, and experiments.

## Default meeting — 90 minutes

### 0–15 — Life / career / business
Each person:
- What changed?
- What are you working through?
- Is there a decision the group can help sharpen?

### 15–25 — Market pulse
One rotating person:
- what changed;
- evidence;
- possible implications;
- what remains uncertain.

### 25–45 — Reading discussion
Everyone brings:
- one idea they agree with;
- one claim they question;
- one implication the author did not explore.

### 45–55 — Broken Assumption / Blooper
Bring a prediction, process, AI output, or market narrative that failed.

Ask:
- What assumption failed?
- What evidence did we miss?
- What mental rule should change?

### 55–80 — Build lab
Open GitHub and complete one concrete piece of work.

### 80–90 — Close
Record:
- decisions;
- actions;
- owner for each action;
- next reading;
- next build target.

## Rotating roles

- **Market Lead** — 5-minute market snapshot.
- **Reading Lead** — chooses/contextualizes the primary source.
- **Builder** — moves the shared project.
- **Skeptic** — asks what might be wrong, missing, or unfalsifiable.

With three members, one person can hold two light roles.

## Norms

- Ask for jargon to be unpacked.
- “I don't know” is useful information.
- Separate facts, estimates, interpretations, and opinions.
- Debate ideas without making it personal.
- Do not expose confidential employer information.
- Every meeting should leave one durable artifact in GitHub.

## Six-month test

By six months, each member should be better able to:
- explain core market plumbing;
- interpret market moves without collapsing them into a headline;
- build and evaluate a small agent;
- collaborate through issues, branches, and PRs;
- reason about where tokenization changes infrastructure and where it does not;
- map a process before trying to automate it;
- show a portfolio of real experiments, including failures.
