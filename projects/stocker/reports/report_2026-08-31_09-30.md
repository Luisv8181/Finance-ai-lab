### 5. Educational Insight

**What are Index Funds (and ETFs)?**

An **index fund** is a type of mutual fund or exchange-traded fund (ETF) with a portfolio constructed to match or track the components of a financial market index, such as the S&P 500 or the Nasdaq 100. Rather than actively picking individual stocks, index funds aim to replicate the performance of a specific index.

*   **Key Characteristics:**
    *   **Diversification:** They offer broad market exposure, reducing single-stock risk.
    *   **Lower Costs:** Typically have lower expense ratios than actively managed funds because they don't require extensive research by fund managers.
    *   **Passive Management:** Their strategy is to "buy and hold" the underlying index components.

ETFs like SPY (which tracks the S&P 500) and QQQ (which tracks the Nasdaq 100) are popular examples of index funds, offering investors an easy way to gain exposure to broad market segments.
